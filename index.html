05 — Mathematical Analysis

The mathematics of bending light

From Fermat's principle of least time to the wave interpretation — the full derivation of Snell's Law.

Snell's Law

n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2
01
Light travels at speed c/n in a medium of refractive index n — slower in denser media.
02
Fermat's Principle: light follows the path that takes the least time between two points, not the shortest distance.
03
Minimizing total travel time across a boundary (via calculus, setting dT/dx = 0) yields exactly n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
04
Equivalently, Huygens' wavefronts pivot at the boundary because one edge of the wavefront slows before the other, rotating its direction.

Refractive index vs. critical angle

Critical angle θc = sin⁻¹(n₂ / n₁), shown here for n₂ = 1 (air). Higher n₁ → smaller critical angle → total internal reflection kicks in sooner.
